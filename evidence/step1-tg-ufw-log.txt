These lines must not be filed as evidence of blocking unauthorised access. They
are this host's OWN outbound traffic being severed by its OWN firewall coming up.

ufw was enabled at 03:35:08–03:35:14 UTC. Every block falls 4–27 seconds later.

Eight of the nine are a single flow: 160.79.104.10:443 -> 10.0.0.173:44544, with
IP IDs incrementing 55275→55282 and intervals backing off 0.25s, 0.26s, 0.46s,
1.0s, 2.0s, 3.8s, 7.9s. That is one server retransmitting into a connection whose
return path had just been cut. 160.79.104.10 is Anthropic; port 44544 was a local
ephemeral port belonging to an established HTTPS connection, present in an `ss`
capture taken before the firewall was enabled:

    ESTAB 10.0.0.173:44544 -> 160.79.104.10:443

Port 44544 was subsequently confirmed gone from the connection table and replaced
by a new connection. The ninth line, 54.167.136.58:8886 -> :41086, is the same
shape: a bare ACK into another pre-existing outbound flow.

Cause: enabling ufw installs a fresh ruleset whose ESTABLISHED,RELATED accept
only matches flows conntrack is tracking under the new rules. Connections
predating the enable lose their return path and die. Outbound is permitted, so
everything reconnected on its own.

Filed unannotated beside a claim about blocking unauthorised access, these read
as nine inbound attacks in the first thirty seconds after the firewall came up.
