T11 — implausible sequence numbers

CAPTURED: 2026-09-10, times UTC

Split deliberately. The refusals ran against the production ledger, because they
write no chain entry and cannot affect it. The accepted case ran against a
throwaway instance, because accepting a digest is permanent on an append-only
chain and would have set a delayed-action trap — see the demonstration below.

================================================================================
1. REFUSALS, AGAINST THE PRODUCTION LEDGER — 05:58:55 UTC
================================================================================

Submitted from the primary, through the real firewall rule, to the live ledger:

    seq 999999  -> {"error":"seq 999999 implies 999891 sequences never seen by
                     this ledger (window 1000); retry after the backlog drains",
                     "ok":false,"permanent":false}

    seq 5000    -> {"error":"seq 5000 implies 4892 sequences never seen by this
                     ledger (window 1000); retry after the backlog drains",
                     "ok":false,"permanent":false}

Both refused, audited as IMPLAUSIBLE_SEQ, and no chain entry written — the
refusal returns before the append.

`permanent: false` is deliberate and matters. After a long outage a genuine new
event can legitimately arrive far ahead of a spool that has not drained. A
permanent refusal would dead-letter a real digest and lose evidence, so the
refusal is retryable and succeeds once the backlog catches up.

================================================================================
2. ACCEPTED CASE, ON A THROWAWAY INSTANCE
================================================================================

A separate ledgerd on loopback:19911 with its own chain file, bootstrapped to
head 58 so it mirrors production's state.

    seq 58,   bootstrap on empty chain      -> {"ok":true,"seq":58}
    seq 200,  inside the window (head 58)   -> {"ok":true,"seq":200}
    seq 5000, outside the window            -> refused, IMPLAUSIBLE_SEQ

Acceptance, refusal and the boundary between them on one instance in one session.
The contrast is what shows the window is discriminating, rather than the ledger
taking whatever arrives.

================================================================================
3. THE PRODUCTION TRAP, DEMONSTRATED RATHER THAN ARGUED
================================================================================

The original plan submitted seq 200 to the PRODUCTION chain, on the reasoning
that a junk digest would surface as a DELETION and that this was the evidence.
That reasoning was wrong, and the error was not in the immediate effect but in
failing to follow the sequence forward.

The production agent was at seq 58 and beats once a minute. It reaches seq 200 in
roughly 140 minutes on its own, and submits the GENUINE digest for its own
record at that sequence. On the throwaway instance, that submission:

    seq 200 again, with its real digest
      -> {"error":"seq 200 already chained with a different digest",
          "ok":false,"permanent":true}

    audit: REFUSED_REWRITE {"chained_digest":"f50ff7ed1ab8a07b...", "seq":200}

`permanent: true`, so the agent dead-letters it. Unattended, hours later:

  - a genuine record goes permanently unwitnessed
  - the ledger's audit log gains the exact signature of a rewrite attack
  - the dead-letter file gains a real digest that was never recorded

All three false, all three permanent on an append-only file, all three landing in
evidence while nobody is watching.

The general hazard, which is the part worth carrying forward: **any accepted
out-of-window digest is a delayed-action collision, at a distance the submitter
chooses.** Sequence numbers only climb, so there is no safe number — only a
farther one. Test data must never be written into a production chain.

"We chose not to write junk into the production chain" is a judgement call. The
refusal above, at the sequence it would have occurred, is evidence.

================================================================================
4. SCOPE
================================================================================

Sections 2 and 3 ran on loopback, one host, a throwaway chain, no network between
the parties. That is adequate for acceptance and refusal logic, which is what T11
tests. It is NOT a demonstration of split custody and must not be presented as
one. Section 1 did cross the network to the production ledger through the real
firewall rule.
